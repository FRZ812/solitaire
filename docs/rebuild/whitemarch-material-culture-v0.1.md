# Whitemarch & Whitewend — Material Culture Rules v0.1

**Purpose:** keep generated characters, items, buildings, and props historically plausible, region-specific, and compatible with deterministic mechanics.

## Regional facts

Whitemarch is a large fortified city built around a river crossing, an iron road, and an inner hill. Its identity comes from barges, tolls, grain storage, guild regulation, civic paperwork, foundries, road forts, wall artillery, military remounts, flood control, and the Whitewend basin's farms and wetlands.

The city uses nested defensive walls, black local stone with white mortar, timber-and-plaster common construction, paved and muddy lanes, cranes and warehouses along the river, large grain vaults, smithing quarters, and professional wall crews. It has the wealth and specialization to field brigandines, partial plate, pikes, arbalests, siege engines, and guild-marked steel, but most residents and adventurers do not own elite harness.

## Working historical horizon

Use Central and Northwestern European material culture from approximately 1400–1475 as the first consistency horizon. This supports Whitemarch's civic crossbows, brigandines, mail, early/full plate among elites, fortified gate systems, guild craft, urban textiles, river trade, and mixed professional militias. Assets interpret that grounding through oil-brush painterly 2D anime; the style may simplify surface detail but may not invent implausible construction.

This is not one-to-one reenactment. A deliberate deviation is allowed only when canon, geography, trade, species, or a defined magical rule explains it.

## Character construction checklist

Each generated character must answer:

1. Where were they raised and where are they equipped now?
2. What work pays for their clothing and gear?
3. What is the season and what gets wet, cold, or dirty?
4. Which garments are linen, wool, leather, mail, plate, or imported cloth?
5. How is the load carried and how does the character reach each tool?
6. Which pieces are repaired, borrowed, issued, inherited, or guild-marked?
7. Does the silhouette show role without oversized fantasy shapes?

## Item construction checklist

Every item record includes:

- primary materials and plausible joinery;
- dimensions/weight band where mechanics care;
- maker and production context;
- intended function;
- maintenance and failure mode;
- region and trade availability;
- required skill or spanning tool;
- magical rule ID, source, cost, and visible behavior if enchanted.

An enchanted item must still work, carry, wear, and break like its base object unless the enchantment definition explicitly changes one of those properties.

## Magic presentation rules

- No ambient glow as a rarity label.
- No arbitrary crystals added to ordinary tools.
- A focus is prepared for a defined working and made from materials that serve that rule.
- Effects leave physical evidence appropriate to the effect.
- Scale is proportional to cost, preparation, and mastery.
- Casting in public creates witness and faction consequences.
- Unknown magic cannot be attempted as if it were an unlocked verb.

## First tracer roster

- **Erran Holt, company warden:** linen gambeson with short riveted-mail sleeves, practical kettle hat, dyed wool coat in muted river blue, ash spear, painted wood-and-hide shield, belt knife, and repaired leather boots.
- **Maud Reed, Whitewend scout:** layered linen and weathered wool, hood, bow glove, yew self bow, utility knife, small quiver, and water-darkened boots; no decorative armor.
- **Tavin Croft, road brigand spearman:** patched wool over an aging jack, repaired ash spear, small plank shield, coarse hood, and mismatched gloves; credible poverty and repair.
- **Odo Pell, crossbow skirmisher:** sleeveless padded jack, light crossbow, belt quiver, goatsfoot lever, short side knife, and practical travel layers rather than a fantastical costume.

No member of the first tracer begins with magic. This establishes the ordinary material baseline against which later magic can feel exceptional.
