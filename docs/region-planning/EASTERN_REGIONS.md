# Eastern Regions Planning Notes

These notes are canon constraints for later region work. They are intentionally
brief for now because central-region design comes first.

## Core Ruling: The East Is Plural

The eastern macro-region is not one empire, one kingdom, or one flattened
"Asian" culture. It is a contested civilizational field inspired by a mixture of
Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Mongol, and related cultural logics. Those influences
should produce several distinct peoples, polities, languages, subraces, military
traditions, religions, trade networks, and rival histories.

No single ruler should stand in for the whole east.

## Political Shape

Eastern regions should include unequal powers rather than balanced game factions:

- Large agrarian states with bureaucracy, canals, tax registers, court factions,
  border armies, and internal rebellions.
- Peninsula or mountain kingdoms that survive by forts, passes, naval skill, or
  terrain advantage.
- Island realms or clan-warrior polities where formal legitimacy and military
  power are not always held by the same office.
- Steppe confederations and horse peoples that may not hold cities for long but
  can destroy supply lines and outmaneuver settled armies.
- Treaty ports, merchant cities, scholar monasteries, protectorates, border
  marches, and semi-independent client states.

War should often be asymmetrical. Some powers brutally outnumber their enemies;
some survive because invasion is logistically miserable; some dominate by navy,
horse mobility, money, terrain, bureaucracy, or religious legitimacy rather than
raw battlefield equality.

## Race And Subrace Constraint

Existing and future race/subrace canon should drive eastern geography, not be
overwritten by it. When eastern polities are designed, their culture, settlement
patterns, ruling classes, prejudices, and conflicts should support those peoples
as load-bearing worldbuilding.

## Player-Facing Doctrine

NPC warnings about the east should sound like local knowledge, not level gating:

- Do not cross a steppe without tribute, guides, or speed.
- A court road may be safe until the governor changes.
- A mountain kingdom may lose open battles and still bury armies in snow.
- An island ruler may bow in poems while warlords collect the coin.

The player may ignore warnings, but the world should enforce them through terrain,
politics, supply, and encounter danger.
